# Micro OS 1.2 — the release notes

`1.2.0` is the release of the machine a person can see and feed. The x86-64
port stops drawing into the text mode of the firmware and gets a real screen;
the network of the system learns to stream a file, to speak HTTPS and to
install a signed program; the port boots from a medium a person can carry and
from a tar that is the whole machine; and the programs of other people arrive
- zlib, sbase, PCRE2 and Lua - as files of `/bin`. The shell fixes of `1.1.2`
are in this release, and the volume, the syscall ABI and `MOX1` do not change.

The board stays the **compatibility port** of ADR 0018: it builds, passes its
device test and receives only the changes that are cheap on this machine. It
gets no TLS and no second core in this release. Many cores are `1.3.0`.

## The screen of x86-64

The port programs the Bochs VBE interface of the VGA of QEMU - the PCI device
`1234:1111`, the I/O ports `0x1CE`/`0x1CF` - to **1280x800, 16 bits per
pixel, RGB565**, which is the format `k_renderer` already drew in (ADR 0023).
The window of the card is a memory BAR found through the configuration space
the network port reads; `paging.c` maps it at `0xFFFFFFFFA0000000` in 2 MiB
pages, above the identity map, and the PDPT of the higher half is shared, so
a `write` of a program draws on the same picture as the boot log.

The console is a grid of cells of the font of the PC, **Spleen 8x16 2.3.0**
(BSD-2-Clause, `third_party/spleen`), generated into a table by
`tools/spleen_font.py`: **160 columns by 50 rows**. The gap between two lines
is a key of `/etc/font`: `font.line_gap` 0, 2 or 4 pixels gives 16, 18 or 20
pixels a line and 50, 44 or 40 rows, and the grid is laid out again from the
cells the console holds. A cell carries its own pair of colours - the paper
in the high nibble and the ink in the low one - so a scroll, a repaint and
the cursor (the two colours traded) keep it.

The colours are the sixteen of the CGA in RGB565, and a command asks for one
with the SGR sequences of a terminal: `ESC[0m`, `1`, `22`, `7`, `27`,
`30`-`37`, `39`, `40`-`47`, `49`, `90`-`97` and `100`-`107`, in the portable
and host-tested `kernel/ansi.c`. The log marks its levels - warn amber, error
red, success green - `ls --color` paints a directory blue and an executable
green, and `console.ink`/`console.paper` of `/etc/console` take a name of the
palette or a number of RGB565. The serial copy stays a plain transcript: an
escape sequence of SGR is dropped before COM1.

A machine whose card has no interface - another card, or `-vga cirrus` -
keeps the **text mode of 80x25** of the firmware, with the attribute byte of
the cell and the same colours rounded to the sixteen of the VGA. The port
chooses the screen at the boot and says which one it chose, and the 80x50
mode with the 8x8 font is not built (see "What this release does not do").

`make x86-vga-smoke` reads the picture with `screendump` (a PPM of 1280x800)
and the text mode with `pmemsave`: it compares the cells with the table of
the font the kernel was built with, finds the cursor, types through
`sendkey`, sets `console.theme light` and `console.ink`/`console.paper`, and
reads the colour of a cell out of the picture.

## The network: a stream, an installer and TLS

The HTTP client has a second face beside `http get`: `struct http_stream`
reads a body of `Content-Length` or a chunked body, follows at most five
redirects, gives up after a budget, and writes the bytes into a sink as they
arrive - a `struct vfs_file`, or a fake in the tests. `wget URL FILE` is the
console face of it, and a second run on a file that is already there sends
`Range: bytes=N-` and continues the download.

`pkg` (ADR 0024) is the installer of x86-64: `sync`, `install`, `remove`,
`list`, `info`, `upgrade`, the pacman short forms (`-Sy`, `-S`, `-R`, `-Q`,
`-Qi`, `-Ql`, `-Qk`, `-Sc`, `-U`, `Syu`) and the flags of an install. The
repository publishes a JSON index and a `.mox` per program, both signed with
Ed25519; the installer checks the size, the SHA-256, the signature and the
header of `MOX1` before a byte reaches `/usr/bin`, and an install is atomic
through the new `vfs_rename` (MicroFS and FAT implement it; a volume without
the operation installs in place and says that the window is not atomic). The
database is one file per package under `/var/pkg`, the cache under
`/var/cache/pkg`, the repositories in `/etc/pkg.conf`.

**TLS** is mbedTLS 3.6.2 (ADR 0024 §3), vendored in `third_party/mbedtls`
with a client-only configuration: TLS 1.2 and 1.3, X.509, ECDHE with ECDSA
or RSA, AES-GCM/CCM, ChaCha20-Poly1305, SHA-1/256/384/512. The platform under
it is the kernel: `kmm` for the allocator, the formatter of the kernel,
`RDRAND` with the timer as the fallback for entropy, the clock of NTP for the
dates, and the roots of `/etc/ssl/certs.pem` read through the VFS. A URL with
`https` is the same client with one more layer, with the chain, the name and
the dates checked; a machine whose clock was never set refuses *before* the
socket is opened, with a sentence that names `ntp`, instead of a silent
success. TLS alone grew the image of x86-64 by about 312 KB (from 352 KB to
672 KB at that merge).

`make x86-net-smoke` generates a test authority and three certificates with
`openssl`, and checks a file over TLS 1.3 and TLS 1.2, the refusal of a wrong
name (`flags 0x4`) and of a wrong authority (`flags 0x8`), the refusal of
`https` before `ntp`, and `pkg` installing a signed program from a repository
served over HTTPS.

## Programs from other people

The recipe of a port is [`docs/porting-programs.md`](../porting-programs.md).
The userland library learned what a portable source expects: an arena of its
own in the bss (`malloc`, `calloc`, `realloc`, `free`), `errno`/`strerror`,
the streams of `fopen`, and - for a language and a calculator - `double` and
the runtime it needs.

| Port | In `/bin` | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| zlib 1.3.1 | `zcat` | a `.gz` of MicroFS inflated to the console or a file; the fixture is `/etc/greeting.gz` and every inflated byte is checked |
| sbase (MIT) | `cat`, `grep`, `head`, `ls`, `tail`, `wc`, `sort`, `find`, `xargs`, `sed` | the commands of a Unix-like machine under their own names: `ls -la`, `grep -r`, `sort -n`, `find -name`, a command over the words of a pipe, a substitution |
| PCRE2 10.42 (BSD-3-Clause WITH PCRE2-exception) | `pcre2grep` | a regular expression of a library: `-i`, `-v`, `-n`, `-o` and `-g N`, with the license in `/etc/licenses/pcre2` |
| Lua 5.4.7 (MIT) | `lua` | an interpreter: a power of a double (`1024.0`), `math.pi`, `%.3f`, the script `/etc/hello.lua`, `io` and `os.time` |
| bc/dc 7.2.0 (BSD-2-Clause) | `bc`, `dc` | an arbitrary-precision calculator: `scale`, `ibase`/`obase`, the math library of `-l`, `2^100`, and the same engine in RPN as `dc` |

The ten tools of sbase are **the commands of the machine**, not a second set
beside the old ones: `/bin/ls`, `/bin/cat`, `/bin/grep`, `/bin/head`,
`/bin/wc` replace the small programs of ADR 0016, and `/bin/tail`,
`/bin/sort`, `/bin/find`, `/bin/xargs` and `/bin/sed` are new. The small
`ls`, `cat`, `grep`, `wc` and `head` of ADR 0016 are not built for x86-64 any
more; `/bin/echo`, `/bin/mkdir`, `/bin/pwd` and `/bin/rm` stay as the small
programs of the tree. What the shell keeps for itself is the state and the
special commands: `cd`, `path`, `df`, `mount`, `fsck`, `stat`, `write`,
`mkdir`, `rm`, `tree`, `kill` and `config`.

The shell looks a bare name in `PATH` **before** its own table: `ls /etc` at
the prompt is `/bin/ls`, the same file `exec /bin/ls /etc` runs, and a command
of the shell is the fallback of a machine without programs (the simulator) or
a file that is not there (the board). `ls` takes the colour of the screen:
`--color[=always|never|auto]` paints a directory blue and an executable green
with the SGR sequences of the console, and the serial copy stays plain.
(`auto` is off: this shell has no terminal to ask about.)

The calculator is another port and the first program whose arithmetic is
larger than a machine word. `/bin/bc` and `/bin/dc` are the same
115,424-byte image; the name it is entered with picks the language, BC or
RPN. The image carries `/etc/bc.bc`, `/etc/dc.dc` and `/etc/licenses/bc`;
`scale=10; 4*a(1)` under `bc -l` prints `3.1415926532`, which is the answer
of GNU bc as well (the `atan` of the math library is truncated at the tenth
digit, not rounded to the `...535` some calculators print), and
`bc -e 2^100` prints the whole 31-digit power. The fuzzers of the release,
the history editor and the `bcl` library are not built: this machine has no
terminal behind descriptor 0 and no line editor.

The programs are also published: **`microos-progs`** is a public repository
of programs with a signed index at
<https://turbososiska304.github.io/microos-progs/>. The index and every
`.mox` carry the Ed25519 signature of the project, the key is
`microos-progs.pub` (and `/etc/pkg/keys/microos-progs.pub` on a machine that
wants its own copy), and a machine with a link adds the repository with
`pkg repo add https://turbososiska304.github.io/microos-progs/` and installs
from it with `pkg -S`. TLS protects the wire, the signature protects the
program: a mirror of the repository with a valid certificate still cannot put
another program in place of a signed one.

The image of a program stopped being bounded by one transfer: `execve` reads
a file of up to `K_EXEC_MAX_IMAGE` (8 MiB) in pieces of `KSYS_MAX_IO`
(64 KiB), and a larger file is `E2BIG` before a byte of it is read. The
interpreter of Lua (168,480 bytes) is the first program past the old 64 KiB
edge and the calculator is 115,424 bytes; `make x86-smoke` boots a disk with a
file of 8 MiB + 1 and checks the refusal, and `tests/cases/test_exec.c`
checks the boundary of the constant. The price is written in
[debts.md](../debts.md): the image is one block of the allocator and one copy
in the window, two copies of it for the time of the load.

> tree this page describes: zlib, PCRE2, the canonical sbase, Lua, bc/dc and
> the shell fixes of `1.1.2` are merged, the version is `1.2.0-rc1`, and the

## The ISO: a medium a firmware can start

`make x86-iso` (ADR 0026) writes `build-x86-iso/microos.iso`: an ISO9660
image with an El Torito catalogue (BIOS) that carries the loader, the signed
kernel and the root image. The build uses the boot loader of the system and
does not vendor one: `grub-mkrescue` when it is installed, `limine`
otherwise (the path that was booted on the machine that wrote the ADR), and
`--bootloader grub|limine` names one.

The root is not a disk: it is the second module of the boot. `loader32.S`
keeps the multiboot information, `src/blockdev_module.c` registers the bytes
of the module as the read-only block device `boot0`, the portable parser
reads its MBR and registers `boot0p1`, and the boot mounts that partition at
`/`. The device refuses a write, MicroFS reports `EIO`, and the shell says
`write: /x: input/output error` - a CD has no second copy. The heap of the
port starts above the last byte of every module, and the loader refuses a
module that overlaps the place the kernel is copied to.

`make x86-iso-smoke` boots the ISO with `-cdrom` and nothing else - no
`-kernel`, no `-initrd`, no disk - and checks the signature of the kernel by
the loader, the mount of `boot0p1`, the program the boot runs from `/bin`,
and the shell with `ls`, `df`, `exec /bin/hello` and `ls /`.

## The tar is the machine

`make x86-user` closes the loop between the artifact and the machine: it
builds the complete machine, builds the release bundle, unpacks
`dist/MicroOS-x86_64-<version>.tar.gz` into `build-x86-user/` and starts
`run.sh` **from the tar**, not from the tree. The tar carries
`loader32.elf`, `microos64.bin`, `x86-all.img` (the disk of `/bin` and
`/etc`), the licenses and a `run.sh` that boots the three with a virtio-net
card on the user network of QEMU (port 8080 forwarded). `make x86-user-serial`
is the same tar in a terminal.

What a person downloads is therefore what the target starts, and the release
workflow builds the complete machine before the bundle, so a missing piece
of the machine is a failed release and not a surprise after the download.

## The shell of 1.1.2

`1.2.0` contains the patch [`1.1.2`](1.1.2.md): the shell's `ls` and `tree`
read a directory through `readdir` in pieces instead of a table of 64 and 16
names;
`shell_resolve` normalises `.`, `..`, `//` and a trailing `/`, so `cd ..`,
`cd .` and `ls ..` work; `help <command>` writes a long detail without the
192-byte line buffer; `path ""` is refused; `umount /proc` and `/dev` are
refused while mounted; `df` of a missing path is `no such file or directory`;
`config` on a machine without a store says so and `config sync` fails instead
of a false `0 saved`; `memtest` repeats an operation until the timer can see
it. The volume, the ABI and `MOX1` do not change, so a machine of `1.1.2`
takes the image as it is.

`1.1.1` and its filesystem fix (a directory that grows past its first block)
are in the line as well; a machine of `1.1.0` or older keeps its volume.

## The board is the compatibility port

The ESP32 build of this release is the board of `1.1.0`: the same `/etc`
systems, the same `progs` partition, the same A/B update over USB with a
rollback. It builds (`make idf-build`), its device test prints `RESULT: PASS`,
and it receives the changes that are cheap on this machine - the `errno` and
`libgcc` of its two programs, and the settings of `/etc` that both machines
keep.

It does **not** receive TLS (its shell has no command that speaks it, and
`net_tls_transport()` is the stub of `components/net/src/tls_stub.c` in the
firmware), the installer (`pkg` is not built for the board; its `progs`
belongs to the firmware), or the framebuffer. The board's screen is the
135x240 panel and its 5x7 font, as it was.

## Many cores: 1.3.0, not 1.2.0

The first phase of ADR 0012 is in the tree and is not extended by this
release: `KERNEL_SMP` (off by default), a `struct k_cpu` and a run queue per
core, `k_cpu_lock()` as the critical section of the port, the host tests and
the soak on two cores under TSan, and the ACPI/MADT processor list of x86-64
with the boot line `2 cpu(s) reported by the firmware, 1 in use`.

The application processors of x86-64 are **not started**: the machine lock,
the per-core GDT/TSS/thread pointer, the trampoline with `INIT`/`SIPI`, the
local APIC timer and the I/O APIC are the next phase, and doing them half way
would boot a second core into a kernel that can corrupt its own tables. That
phase is `1.3.0`.

## What this release does not do

The ports that are not here are the `1.3.0` line, and the reason is the same
in three of the four: a program is trusted by the repository's signature and
a package is still bounded by `PKG_PACKAGE_MAX` (1 MiB), which is smaller than
the 8 MiB an image may be.

* **No SQLite.** It needs its own SDK (the shell and the headers of the
  library) and a package above `PKG_PACKAGE_MAX`; its branch is still in
  progress, and the database is `1.3.0`.
* **No zstd.** Its branch is still in progress; a compressed file is `zcat`
  and gzip in this release, and zstd waits for the merge and for a package
  that fits the installer.
* **No `file` and no libmagic.** The branch is still in progress, and the
  database of magic is a size the installer cannot carry yet.
* **No awk.** Its branch is still in progress; `sed`, `sort`, `grep` and
  `xargs` of sbase are the text tools of `1.2.0`.

* **No second core on a machine.** The APs of x86-64 are not started and the
  board does not use `KERNEL_SMP`; the shape and the host gate are there, the
  bring-up is `1.3.0` (ADR 0012).
* **No TLS on the board.** The copy of ESP-IDF is there and no command of the
  board's shell speaks it (ADR 0018).
* **No UEFI, no hybrid ISO and no persistent ISO.** The medium is BIOS only,
  written to a CD or an ISO file; an ISO on a USB stick does not boot as a
  disk, and the root of the CD is read-only - a person's files need a disk.
* **No 80x50 text mode.** The fallback of the framebuffer is the 80x25 of the
  firmware; the 8x8 font load and the CRTC timings are a debt of their own.
* **No directory beyond four kilobytes of names.** MicroFS keeps the names
  of one directory in 4 KiB; a full directory is `no space left on device`
  and not a corruption, and the limit is in
  [`docs/wiki/files.md`](../wiki/files.md).
* **No `xargs -P`.** `xargs` runs its commands one at a time; a parallel
  mode is not a promise of this release.
* **No wall clock in a program.** `time()` of a program is the uptime, not
  the seconds of Unix: the kernel keeps the wall clock and the ABI has no
  call that hands it to a program yet.
* **No certificate revocation.** The store is one file,
  `/etc/ssl/certs.pem`; a withdrawn certificate stays trusted until the file
  is replaced. There is no CRL, no OCSP and no package of certificates.
* **No program of the board beyond the two of the firmware.** Its `/bin` is
  the `progs` partition; a new program is a new `progs.bin` and a flash.

## Upgrading

The syscall ABI, `MOX1` and the format of a MicroFS volume do not change: a
program and a volume of `1.1.x` work. The board keeps its flash layout of
1.1 (two application slots, `progs` and 5.56 MiB of MicroFS); a board of 1.0
still moves its volume once, as the notes of
[`1.1`](1.1.md#upgrading-a-board-of-10-the-volume-is-lost-once) say.

On x86-64 the image is bigger (mbedTLS is about 312 KB), so a machine that
boots the kernel with `-kernel` and `-initrd` keeps working, and a machine of
the ISO or of the tar takes the new medium whole. The settings of `/etc` are
files as before; `/etc/ssl/certs.pem` is a new file, and a machine without it
trusts no authority and says so.

## The checks of the release

```bash
make test test-asan test-tsan   # the host, one core and two
make test-smp                   # the scheduler of two cores
make smoke                      # the simulator
make x86-smoke                  # the machine, the programs and exec
make x86-disk-smoke             # two boots on one disk
make x86-vga-smoke              # the framebuffer, the text mode and the keyboard
make x86-net-smoke              # the network, TLS and the installer
make x86-iso-smoke              # the ISO with -cdrom and nothing else
make integrity                  # the bundle, the table, the layers, hygiene, format
make wiki-check                 # the links of docs/wiki
make idf-build                  # the firmware of the board
tools/device-test.sh /dev/ttyACM0            # the board
DEVICE_OTA=1 tools/device-test.sh /dev/ttyACM0   # the update and the rollback
```

The gates of the line are the ones of `1.0.0` and `1.1.0`, on this tree: a
session of 600 seconds on each machine and the host substrate under TSan.
They are run **after the integration**, when the merged tree is the tree of
the release; the table below is filled from those runs and not from a wish.

| Gate | Command | Result |
|---|---|---|
| x86-64, 600 s | `make soak` | 600.7 s, 260 cycles, 9719 commands, 0 errors, 0 warnings |
| the board, 180 s | `make board-soak` | 182.3 s, 15 cycles, 515 commands, 0 errors, 0 warnings |
| host, two cores, TSan, 600 s | `make soak-host` | not run by the integration (it is the host half, not a machine of the release) |

The three sessions are one boot each. `make soak` builds the image of
`tools/soak_image.py` - a full volume is part of the load - and drives the
machine for 600 seconds; `make soak-host` runs the portable kernel of two
cores under the thread sanitizer and valgrind; `make board-soak` drives the
board over its console for **three minutes** (`BOARD_SOAK_MINUTES`, the gate
of a compatibility release: the board of 1.2 is ADR 0018 and the changes are
small). A session that touches the flash or the boot asks for the long one
with `BOARD_SOAK_MINUTES=10`. A run is the numbers of its transcript (cycles,
commands, errors, warnings), and the table above takes them when the runs are
made.
