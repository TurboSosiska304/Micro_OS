# Micro OS 1.9

> The release of the machine that owns its files: a wake that costs
> microseconds and not a tick, a journal so that a power cut costs the last
> write and nothing else, a guard page so that a fault of a task is not a
> fault of the machine, a terminal that a program of the outside world can own,
> and a machine that switches itself off.

1.8 made a laptop work: a keyboard and a mouse of the USB, a stick as a disk, a
disk that keeps a file, a library that a program of POSIX compiles against with
no port layer. 1.9 is the release of what happens when the machine is asked to
be **trusted**: the file after the power cut, the fault of one program, the
microsecond a message waits, and the order in which a machine goes off.

## The wake that preempts (ADR 0046)

The one defect the ABI had carried since 1.0: a wake moved `wake_tick` to the
present, and the waiter became runnable at the next tick of the timer - a round
trip of a pipe was **1.006 ms**, one tick, on the machine and on the host. The
waiter **arms** its record under the lock of the object, looks at the object,
and only then sleeps; the wake takes it out of the queue of the sleeping tasks
and runs it, and the port's sleep is cut by the wake itself.

| | 1.8.0 | 1.9.0 |
|---|---|---|
| `lat_pipe_p50`, x86-64 in QEMU | 1 005 056 ns | **19 968 ns** |
| `lat_pipe_p99`, x86-64 in QEMU | 1 044 992 ns | **46 592 ns** |
| a round trip of a pipe, host | 1.006 ms | **3 µs** |

The twenty milliseconds of chunk that 1.8 put on the waits of a pipe and of
`poll` - the bandage of K15 - are gone: the wake cannot be lost, so the waiter
does not have to look again.

## The journal: a file is whole or is not there (ADR 0047)

A volume of **version 4** commits the metadata of a change through a
write-ahead log in `META`: a header, a slot per block, the commit and its
flush, then the home blocks, then a checkpoint. A draft that was never
committed is removed at the mount and its blocks are free; a committed one is
replayed. Volumes of versions 1 to 3 mount exactly as they always did.

The promise is held by two gates:

* `tests/cases/test_mfs_journal.c` cuts a file of 48 KiB at **every one** of
  its 221 points of a write, and every point of a `rename`: the mount, then
  `mfs_check` with nothing to repair, the file before the cut whole, and the
  draft **whole or absent**;
* `make x86-cut-smoke` cuts a machine that is writing 6 MiB at **twenty random
  points of the write it measured** (a share of its own duration, so a fast
  host cannot miss the middle): the volume is consistent every time, the file
  that was already there is whole, and the draft is whole or absent.

The price is inside the noise of the medium: **5.18 MiB/s** written on a
version 3 volume against **5.25** on a version 4 one, and the `META` of a
volume of 5 GiB grows from 660 blocks to 1973 (1.9 MB, 0.04 %).

## The guard of a stack (ADR 0048)

A kernel task whose stack overflowed walked into the heap under it; the
fault arrived much later and somewhere else - `docs/debts.md` has the story of
the 16 KiB stack of the shell under `pkg -S`, whose fault came in
`k_sched_tick`. The stack of every task of the kernel has a guard page now, on
x86-64 and on the host: the fault is recognised by its address and it ends
**that task**, and the machine answers the next line. A program of ring 3 has
the same page, and the audit of `exec` refuses an image that asks for a page
that is both writable and executable.

`make x86-smoke` is 412 checks, and `/bin/crash stack` is the program that
proves the end of the task and not of the machine.

## The terminal and a program we did not write (ADR 0051)

`/dev/tty` with `termios`: `tcgetattr`, `tcsetattr`, `cfmakeraw`, `VMIN` and
`VTIME`, `TCGETS`/`TCSETS`, `TIOCGWINSZ`, and an `isatty` that asks. The
descriptors 0, 1 and 2 of a task are its terminal while nothing is opened on
them, the mode belongs to the terminal, and the console returns to canonical
when the program that changed it goes away. No new syscall was needed:
`KSYS_IOCTL` has existed since 1.0, and `struct termios` is 36 bytes, byte for
byte the one of Linux.

On it, **kilo** - a full-screen editor of somebody else (BSD-2, commit
`323d93b`, **not one line changed**) is built by the SDK and driven at the
machine: open a file, type a line, `Ctrl-S`, `Ctrl-Q`, and the line is in the
volume. `make tty-smoke` is 12 checks and `make ports-smoke` is 73.

The screenshot of that screen found a real defect of the console: it wrapped
on the **write** of the last cell, so a status bar exactly as wide as the
screen scrolled the frame once per redraw. The wrap is deferred to the next
character now.

## The machine that switches itself off (ADR 0050)

A machine that cannot be switched off is a machine whose files are whatever the
power cut left. `shutdown`, `poweroff` and `reboot` are commands, and the order
of a clean end is: the sessions are closed to new ones, the volumes are synced
and the disks flushed (`FLUSH CACHE EXT`, the NVMe `Flush`) **before** any task
is signalled, then `SIGTERM`, a second sync for what the tasks wrote, the
unmount of every volume, and the power. A flush that fails **refuses** the
shutdown and says why.

The port grew `k_port_power()` (contract 1.13): ACPI `S5` through the FADT on
x86-64, with the ports of QEMU and Bochs and the reset register behind it; deep
sleep on the board, with `HOLD` kept and the button as the wake; a documented
exit code on the host. `make power-smoke` proves it: the process of QEMU ends
by itself, the same image boots again with a clean volume and its file, and
`reboot` boots the same process a second time.

## The defects the release found

* the console wrapped at the write of the last cell, and a full-screen program
  scrolled its frame once per redraw (found by a screenshot of kilo);
* a read of a record inside its own transaction saw the record as free
  (found by the host test of the journal);
* the cut gate of the journal fired at a moment measured by the wall clock and
  missed the middle of a fast write, blaming the file for the gate;
* the gate of the release itself ran beside the builds of three lanes and
  reported a lost console as a defect of the machine: it waits for a quiet
  host now, and says how long it waited.

## What is not in 1.9

* **ext2 with a journal** (the subset of ext3): the form and the criteria are
  in `docs/debts.md`, and a journal in half of the paths is worse than none;
* `fsync`: a file is published by `close` and not by "these bytes are on the
  medium";
* a file changed **in place** is a draft like a new one: a cut removes it and
  does not bring the old version back (the chain is not copied on write);
* `SIGWINCH`, the arrows of the console in the stream of characters, NAWS in
  the terminal of telnet, and colours beyond the sixteen attributes of a cell;
* `vi` and `neatvi` - the next step of the terminal, after the arrows and a
  termcap;
* the **group** and the `setuid` bit of the accounts (ADR 0044 has the six
  steps), Ethernet with DHCP, and the ARM port (`docs/plan-arm64.md`).
