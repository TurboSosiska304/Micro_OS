# Micro OS 1.4

The release of the **root that is not MicroFS** and of the **programs of
somebody else**. A machine may boot from ext2 or FAT32; the installer brings
copyleft programs from a repository that carries their source; the volumes grow
past a gigabyte; and the screen has the first shape of an interface.

## The root is what the medium says

ADR 0027: the type of the root is read, not built in. The partition type of the
MBR is a hint, the magic of the superblock decides, and the registry of the VFS
breaks a tie. The boot line says what it found:

```text
root: the root is ext2 on ata0p1
the programs come from ata0p1:/bin, the volume is ext2
```

A medium no filesystem of the image recognises is refused with a sentence that
names the partition and its first bytes - not a panic and not a shell over an
empty tree. The release carries two images of the same tree (`x86-all.img` on
MicroFS and `x86-ext2.img` on ext2 with a FAT32 partition beside it), `run.sh`
chooses with `X86_ROOT=ext2`, and `make x86-root-smoke` runs the same commands
on both.

## A volume of gigabytes

The map of the free blocks of MicroFS is allocated for the volume the mount
finds (16 MiB of image: 2 KiB of map; 5 GiB: 615 KiB), and ext2 keeps its
bitmaps on the medium. A 5 GiB ext2 volume is created, mounted as `/`, written,
read and checked by `e2fsck` on the host; the numbers of the write are in the
notes of the release.

## Copyleft, with its source

`microos-progs` now carries programs under GPL: each one is built by the **SDK**
(`make sdk`: a sysroot, `libmicroos_user.a`, the crt, the linker script and a
CMake toolchain file), published with its exact source, the patches of its port,
its license texts and a signed index. `pkg` refuses a copyleft package that
names no source (ADR 0025, `PKG_ELICENSE`).

## The interface

ADR 0028: the interface is a program with two devices. `/dev/fb0` takes a
rectangle of pixels and answers the shape of the picture; `/dev/input0` gives
the keys as events; `FBIO_TAKE` and `FBIO_GIVE` hand the screen over and back,
and a panic always takes it. `/bin/ui` draws a panel (the time, the tasks, the
volume), a list of windows with a focus and a window of the console log, and
repaints only what changed. `Tab` moves the focus, `q` returns to the shell.

## The defects of 1.3.0 that the release found

* `execve` waited for a program with a budget of ten seconds and **returned**,
  leaving the task running: under load the shell reported a failure and the
  task stayed in the table for ever. The budget is a minute, and a program
  that outlives it is stopped and reaped.
* the skip of a field the installer does not know read the opening quote of a
  string as its closing one, so one `homepage` of an index made the whole
  document unreadable.
* `rename` did not resolve a relative name, while `open`, `stat` and `unlink`
  did: a program that saves through a temporary name lost the file.

## What is not in 1.4

* USB: the base of the bus arrives in `1.4.0-rc2`;
* a journaled ext2, `rename` and `rmdir` in the VFS, the repair of ext2;
* the users: accounts and `su` are the work of 1.5;
* the I/O APIC, the FPU state of a task, a guard page (the K8 table of
  `docs/debts.md`).
