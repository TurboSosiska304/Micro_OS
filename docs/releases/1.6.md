# Micro OS 1.6

The release where the **machine is in the network**, a **program maps the
memory it asks for**, a task has a **user**, the **pipes run at the speed of
the memory** and the **disk leaves the 1990s behind**.

The board stays the compatibility port of ADR 0018: it builds, passes its
device test and receives the cheap changes. Everything below that names a
processor is x86-64 or the host substrate.

## A program maps its memory

`mmap`, `munmap` and `msync` (44 to 46) and `brk` (47), with the memory of a
**region of the address space** and not of a task: the pages are taken on the
fault from the filesystem, so nothing is read before it is touched.

* `MAP_PRIVATE` takes the page on the first write and the file does not see
  it; `MAP_SHARED` marks the page dirty and `msync`, `munmap` and the end of
  the program write it back through the VFS. `munmap` refuses a piece of a
  region before it writes anything.
* the **image of a program is not a copy**: `execve` reads 32 bytes of the
  header and maps the text, the data and the bss as three regions of the file.
  A program of 4 MiB runs with the heap of the kernel unchanged before, during
  and after it - 13 965 KB in `/bin/big` of `make x86-smoke`.
* a **guard page** under the stack of a program: the page is never mapped, the
  heap cannot cross it, and a write there is `EFAULT` and a fault of the
  program.
* `make x86-root-smoke` reads a shared mapping back with `cat` and the host's
  `e2fsck` and `debugfs` see the bytes it wrote (50 checks, two roots).

`mmap` of 64 MiB, `MAP_FIXED`, `mprotect` and a page cache are the work of the
next release and are named in `docs/debts.md`.

## A user is a task, and the console has a login

`/etc/passwd` (`name:uid:gid:home:shell`) and `/etc/shadow`
(`name:$sha256$salt$digest`, mode 0600, `*` and `!` for a locked account),
`getpwnam` and `getpwuid` read the same file as the shell, and the console has
`login`, `su` and `id` (ADR 0033).

* `login` asks three times, then refuses; a right answer is the home, then
  `setgid` and `setuid` in one critical section of the scheduler.
* `su` lets root become anybody without a password and a user become another
  only with the password of that account; `su -` enters the home.
* a password is never echoed and the prompt of the shell does not change.
* **there is no setuid bit in `MOX1` and no group in a record of the
  filesystem**: the console is the door and both are a format change with an
  ADR of their own. The paragraph of `docs/debts.md` says what it costs.

The cleanroom of this line found two defects of the permissions and both are
fixed here: **the write bit of a directory** is checked for create, unlink,
rmdir and rename (a task of uid 7 deleted `/etc/passwd` and wrote its own
`/etc/shadow` - three commands to root), and a **cwd that does not fit is
refused and not cut** (a name cut at 31 characters is another directory, and
`rm` there removed the file of somebody else).

## The many cores take their interrupts

ADR 0034: the **I/O APIC** replaces the 8259 for the timer and the keyboard -
the table of the MADT with the overrides of the ISA lines, one entry per line,
the destination core in the entry, the EOI to the local APIC of the core that
took the line - and the 8259 stays as the fallback of a machine without one.

The scheduler now **wakes the core that was given a task** (`k_port_cpu_wake`,
one IPI, vector 0x51) instead of waiting for its tick. `smp wake` proves it by
stopping the clock of the other core: the only way a task begins there is the
interrupt.

The 600-second session found the bug of the first version: the body of the IPI
switched in the window where a task has left the scheduler but has not yet
published its stack and its address space, and each hit leaked 32 KiB and its
page tables (`fork` answered `ENOSPC` after ~160 cycles). The body refuses the
window now, as the tick has done since 1.3.0, and the session is clean:
**PASS 600 s**, 0 errors.

## The disk is not of the 1990s

ADR 0032 is the contract of a **physical address** a device may be given (the
kernel image, the heap, the page of a program; a piece that crosses a page is
cut and translated piece by piece; a page that cannot be translated falls back
to PIO). On it:

* **bus-master DMA** on the PCI IDE function, found by its class, with a PRDT
  of 4 KiB pieces and IRQ 14 instead of a poll of the status register;
* a **cache of 512 KiB** above the block layer, write-back, with its state in
  `/proc/blockdev`;
* `rep insl`/`rep outsl` (32-bit PIO) instead of the loop of 16-bit words.

| Volume of 5 GiB | 1.5.0 | 1.6.0 |
|---|---|---|
| ext2, write 128 MiB | 8.2 MiB/s | **14.4 MiB/s** |
| ext2, read 128 MiB | 13.5 MiB/s | **35.9 MiB/s** |
| MicroFS, write 16 MiB | 3.5 MiB/s | **8.6 MiB/s** |
| MicroFS, read 16 MiB | 17.2 MiB/s | **77.3 MiB/s** |
| `fsck` of 5 GiB | 11.9 s | **1.8 s** |

DMA gave **nothing on writing** - PIO already kept up with the filesystem -
and three times on reading. The ceiling of the writing is no longer the bus:
ext2 asks the device for **11 sectors of a kilobyte for every kilobyte of a
file** (the data, the zeroing of a new block, the bitmap of the group, its
descriptor, the inode). That is the lane of 1.7: a block of 4 KiB, runs of
blocks, and no zeroing of a block that is about to be written.

## The pipes and the streams

The ring of a pipe was **256 bytes** and the stream of a program had **no
buffer**: a megabyte through `cat | wc` took 5.9 seconds, and 0.20 MiB/s was
the pipe itself. Both are gone.

* the ring is `KFILE_PIPE_SIZE` - **64 KiB** on x86-64 and the host, **4 KiB**
  on the board - taken from the allocator when the pipe is made and given back
  by the last close, with the atomicity of `PIPE_BUF` kept;
* the wait of a pipe is the **channel of the scheduler**: the writer wakes the
  reader, the reader wakes the writer, a close wakes both, and no task spins a
  millisecond;
* `setvbuf` does something now: line-buffered on a terminal, 4 KiB block on a
  file and a pipe, `_IONBF` for stderr, `fflush`/`fclose`/`exit` write.

| 1 MiB | before | 1.6.0 |
|---|---|---|
| `pipe` | 0.20 MiB/s | **31.25 MiB/s** |
| `cat file \| wc -c` | 5.9 s | **0.4 s** |
| `syscall` (2 M calls) | 0.92 µs | **0.55 µs** |

## The system call takes the fast door

ADR 0035: the library calls with `syscall` and returns with `sysret`
(`IA32_STAR`, `LSTAR`, `FMASK`, `SCE` on every core), the entry finds the
kernel stack of the program in the per-core record under `IA32_GS_BASE`, and
the frame it builds is the same 160 bytes in the same order as the vector
0x80 leaves - so the signal frame, the child of a `fork` and the replacement
frame of an `execve` did not move. `sysret` is guarded: a frame that is not
canonical or not ring 3 goes back through `iretq`, because the frame of a
`sigreturn` comes from memory the program can write. The vector 0x80 stays as
the second door and the programs of the port still use it, so every run
exercises both.

## The machine is in the network

* **`httpd serve [port] [root]`**: GET and HEAD, keep-alive, `Content-Length`,
  `Content-Type`, `ETag` and `304`, one `Range` (`206`, `416`), `301` for a
  directory without its slash, a directory as a chunked listing, `405` for the
  rest. `..` - `%2e%2e` too - is `403`, and a directory is never served as
  bytes. A line per request in `/var/log/httpd.log`. The sessions are tasks:
  **3 on x86-64, 3 on the board, 16 on the host**, and the one past the limit
  is an honest `503`, not a dropped SYN.
* **TLS on the serving side**: `MBEDTLS_SSL_SRV_C` in the vendored 3.6.2, the
  certificate and the key from `/etc/ssl`, TLS 1.3 negotiated, `curl -k`
  reading the file of the guest byte for byte.
* **an update over the network with a rollback** (ADR 0036): the image goes to
  a staging file, the trailer and the SHA-256 of the payload (read again from
  the file, not from the stream) and the Ed25519 signature are checked **before
  the write**, then the payload goes into the inactive slot. The states are
  the ones of the board's bootloader - `NEW`, `PENDING`, `VALID` - and a boot
  that finds a `PENDING` of the last time **rolls back**. `make
  x86-net-smoke` proves the whole circle, a corrupted image among it.

The board takes its update over **HTTP and a signature**, not HTTPS: mbedTLS
does not fit in the 1.125 MiB slot (134 KiB free), and the rule of ADR 0024 §4
is that a package is trusted by its signature and not by its transport.

## The installer speaks HTTPS on a fresh image

The cleanroom of this line could not install a package from the documented
repository on a fresh image. Five causes, all of them fixed: the image carried
no store of roots; **SHA-384 was missing** from the configuration of mbedTLS
(so an ECDSA P-384 root was unreadable); a positive answer of the parser was
read as a fatal one; `HTTP_HEAD_MAX` of 640 bytes against the 702 of GitHub
Pages; and the key of the image was not the key of the repository.

A fresh image now does `pkg repo add https://turbososiska304.github.io/microos-progs/`,
`pkg -Sy` reports six packages, and `pkg -S hello` installs it.

## The rest of the list of the cleanroom

* a pipeline is as long as the task table allows and **says so before it
  starts**: a stage of a program is two tasks, so the documented maximum is
  15 stages of programs and 16 commands of the shell, and the arithmetic is in
  `docs/wiki/shell.md`;
* `pipe()` no longer hands out the descriptors 0, 1 and 2: a stage keeps its
  ends above them, and the end that used to land on stdout is why a pipeline
  of 15 stages sometimes had no count;
* `mkdir a b`, `df` on FAT (`512 bytes each`, "not kept by this volume"),
  `kill -TERM` of the shell of the console, `file name too long` in eight
  commands, `login` with an empty name;
* a machine built with `KERNEL_EXEC=0` compiles again (`make test-minimal` was
  red since 1.5).

## The numbers, and how to take them

`make bench` is the new gate of the table: `syscall`, `fork+exec`, the pipe,
the file, the console, each of a fixed size, with a JSON form and a warning
when the host was too busy to be a number. `docs/bench.md` carries the method,
the baseline of 1.5.0 and what is not covered (latency, `poll`, the network,
the board, one sample per run).

## What is not in 1.6

* MQTT is stopped for the x86 port (the decision of 23 November 2026): no
  broker, no topics, no QoS; the client of `components/net/` stays on the
  shelf;
* `/bin/httpd` is not a `MOX1` program yet: the ABI has no socket call (the
  next free number is 48) and the network is the contract of the kernel;
* HTTPS on the board, the flash path of its update on real hardware, a page
  cache, `MAP_FIXED`, `mprotect`, a socket in the ABI;
* the block of 4 KiB and the runs of blocks of 1.7, and the journal of ext2.
