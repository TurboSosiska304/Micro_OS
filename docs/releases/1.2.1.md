# Micro OS 1.2.1

`1.2.1` is a patch of [`1.2.0`](1.2.md). Three defects of the release are
fixed - two of them could corrupt the data of a program - and two of the
things a person asked for at the prompt are now true: a word may be quoted,
and `help` describes the machine that is running. The volume, the syscall ABI
and `MOX1` do not change; a machine of `1.2.0` takes the image as it is.

## A stream reads and seeks like a stream

`fread` took the byte of `ungetc` through a pointer it advanced and then wrote
the rest of the block at `bytes + got`, where `got` already counted that byte:
the data landed one byte past its place and one byte past the buffer of the
caller. A program that reads a line with `getc` and then a block with `fread`
- which is what a `sed` or a `grep` does - wrote over its own memory.

`fseek(SEEK_CUR)` asked the descriptor, and the block of read-ahead of `1.2.0`
puts the descriptor ahead of the stream by the bytes the reader has not taken:
a relative seek after a short read moved by a whole block. `ftell` had the
same hole for the byte of `ungetc`.

Neither could be seen from the host: a stream needs a kernel under it. The
machine now carries `/bin/streams`, a program that gives a byte back, reads a
word, and seeks by two; `make x86-smoke` runs it and wants the line
`streams: ok`.

## The words of the shell

A word may be quoted: `'...'` is a literal, `"..."` keeps its spaces and
understands `\"` and `\\`, and `\` outside the quotes escapes the next
character. The operators of the line - `|`, `>`, `>>`, `<`, `2>` - are
operators only outside the quotes, so `awk '{print $1}' file`,
`grep 'a b' file`, `sed 's/a b/c d/'` and `echo "a  b"` (two spaces) are the
lines they look like. An unclosed quote is refused with a sentence, not a
crash.

## The `help` that describes this machine

`help` listed the commands of the shell and nothing else: `pkg`, `wget`, `bc`,
`lua`, `zcat` were invisible, and `help ls` described the builtin while the
machine ran `/bin/ls`. Now the list ends with the programs of `PATH`, and
`help NAME` says both things when both are true: `NAME is a program:
/bin/NAME` and, under it, the builtin the shell falls back to.

## `make x86-user` without the firmware toolchain

The target that boots the tar a person downloads built the ESP-IDF firmware
first, so a machine without ESP-IDF could not produce that artifact at all.
The packing is two halves now - `tools/bundle.sh --x86-only` and
`--board-only` - with `make bundle-x86`, `make bundle-board` and a `bundle`
that composes them; the release workflow keeps its one command.

## The check that would have caught the `1.2.0` changelog

The `CHANGELOG` of `1.2.0` shipped with the markers of the merge that wrote it
and every check passed, because none of them looked. A line that starts with
the marker of an unfinished merge now fails `make integrity`, with the file
and the line.

## The checks

```bash
make test test-asan test-tsan        # the host: 4/4, 64 894 checks
make x86-smoke                       # 248
make x86-disk-smoke x86-net-smoke x86-vga-smoke x86-iso-smoke   # 42 / 57 / 69 / 14
make smoke                           # the simulator: 69
make x86-user                        # the tar, without ESP-IDF
make soak                            # 600 seconds
make integrity
make idf-build
tools/device-test.sh /dev/ttyACM0    # the board: PASS
```

The patch changes the library of a program, the words of the shell and the
build; the filesystem, the scheduler, the ABI and the port are the ones of
`1.2.0`, whose 600-second sessions remain the evidence of the line.
