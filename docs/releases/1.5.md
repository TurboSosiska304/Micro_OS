# Micro OS 1.5

The release where a program is a **process**, the filesystem is **complete**,
a task has a **user**, the screen has **programs** and the floating point of a
task is **saved**.

The board stays the compatibility port of ADR 0018: it builds, passes its
device test and receives the cheap changes. Everything below that names a
processor is x86-64 or the host substrate.

## A program is a process

Until 1.4 a program started from the shell was one task created by the port,
and `execve` was a call the caller waited out. The four shapes of POSIX are
there now (M1 of the 1.5 plan):

* **`fork()`** answers 0 in the child and the pid in the parent, and the child
  has a copy of the address space - copy-on-write, one page at a time, the way
  ADR 0005 and ADR 0007 describe it;
* **`execve()` from a program replaces the image of the caller**: the port
  rewrites the frame of the call (RIP, RSP, RDI, RSI, RDX, the rest zeroed),
  switches CR3 and lets `iretq` enter the new image. The pid, the parent, the
  descriptors and the directory stay; the handlers of caught signals go back
  to their default;
* **`waitpid()`** reaps the child and answers its status, and **`-1`** means
  "any child";
* a **pipeline is no longer three stages**: the parser holds sixteen (the most
  a line of 32 words can carry), the pipes are made one boundary at a time as
  the stages are forked, and the system has sixteen pipes and forty open-file
  descriptions;
* **`xargs -P`** works: the command of sbase forks a child per batch, the
  child's `execve` replaces it, and the parent reaps with `waitpid(-1)` without
  blocking below the ceiling of `-P`.

The shell itself stays the synchronous caller it was: it is a kernel task with
no address space to replace, and the port creates the program as a task of its
own and waits for it. That is recorded in `docs/debts.md` as a decision and not
as a hole: the POSIX pair belongs to a program, and `/bin/sh` will be one.

`/bin/procs` proves the shapes in `make x86-smoke`: a child that computes 55
and returns it, a double fork with two statuses, a child whose `execvp`
becomes another program, a line of ten stages and six commands under
`xargs -P 4`.

### The two defects this found

* the parent's first write after a `fork()` went into the frame the child was
  about to return into: the clone made both page tables read-only but flushed
  the TLB with an address 32 MiB above the page (the base of the user window
  was counted twice), so the stale writable entry stayed. The clone flushes the
  address it changed;
* `waitpid(-1)` and `wait()` could not wait for any child: the pid arrives in
  an unsigned register, where -1 is larger than every pid, and the scan
  compared it as a number. The scan compares the signed value.

## A user is a task

The triads of a file were checked against a constant. A task carries a **uid**
and a **gid** now, inherited by `task_create`, by `fork` and kept by `execve`,
and the VFS compares them:

| Call | Number | Who may |
|---|---|---|
| `getuid`, `geteuid`, `getgid` | 36, 37, 38 | anyone |
| `setuid`, `setgid` | 39, 40 | root to any value of 15 bits; a user only to the one it has |

Root (uid 0) passes every triad; anyone else gets the bits of the owner or of
"others" - a filesystem of this system stores no group, so the middle triad is
never read. `chmod` is the owner's or root's, `chown`, `mount` and `umount` are
root's, and `kill` of a task of another uid answers `EPERM`. `execve` needs one
of the three `x` bits, which is the POSIX exception for root.

One credential and not three: `geteuid` equals `getuid`, there is no saved uid
and no setuid bit in `MOX1`. `setuid` does not come back. Accounts, `/etc/passwd`,
`login` and `su` are the second half, and `docs/debts.md` holds the reason: a
filesystem record has no group and an image has no setuid bit.

## The filesystem to the end

* **`rename`** reaches ext2 now (MicroFS and FAT32 had it), a file moves into
  any directory of its volume and replaces what it finds, a directory moves
  only inside its own parent (`KERR_NOTSUP`), and the new name is written
  before the old one is removed - a failure leaves a second name, never a lost
  object;
* **`rmdir`** is a call of its own, **41**, in the VFS and in all three
  filesystems: `unlink` of a directory is `KERR_ISDIR`, `rmdir` of a file is
  `KERR_NOTDIR`, a full directory is `KERR_NOTEMPTY`. `rm -r` and `/bin/rmdir`
  use it;
* **`fstat`**, **42**, answers the mode, the owner, the size and the two
  moments of the object behind an open descriptor, instead of the size through
  `lseek` and a made-up 0666;
* **`O_EXCL`** is a flag of `open` and of the ABI;
* **`ext2_repair`** and **`fsck ext2 [-y] <point>`**: the counters of the
  superblock and of every group, both bitmaps, the link counts, the `.` and
  `..` entries and the size of a directory against its chain, and a lost file
  reconnected as `#<inode>` under `/lost+found`. The oracle is the host:
  `e2fsck -fn` answered `Free blocks count wrong` with exit 4 before the repair
  and exit 0 after it;
* the map of **`mfs_check`** is sized by the volume and not by a constant of
  32 768 records: a volume of 40 000 records repairs an orphan past the old
  ceiling;
* **SQLite keeps its journal in a file** (`journal_mode=DELETE`) instead of the
  memory of the process: `make x86-fs-smoke` cuts a transaction with a reset
  and reads the rolled-back rows on the next boot, `PRAGMA integrity_check`
  answering `ok`.

## The programs of the screen

ADR 0028 gave a program two devices in 1.4.0; 1.5 gives it the programs.

* **`components/ui`** is a portable library, tested on the host: a window is a
  rectangle in the order of the paint with an id, a focus and a modal flag; a
  canvas fills, outlines and lays out a line of cells of a font the caller
  gives; the keyboard maps the names of `/dev/input0` to the characters of the
  US layout with Shift, Ctrl, Alt and Caps.
* **`/bin/edit`** is a text editor: a file from argv, the arrows, Home, End,
  PgUp, PgDn, Insert, Delete, ASCII with Shift and Caps, `Ctrl+S` to save and
  `Ctrl+Q` to leave, a title bar, a status line with the position and the
  bytes, a modal window that asks about an unsaved file, and a repaint of the
  rows and the cell that changed and no others.
* **`/bin/fm`** is a file manager: the directory through `readdir`, sorted, the
  selection, Enter to enter a directory or hand a file to `/bin/edit`, `d` or
  Delete to `unlink` after a modal question, `q` to return.
* the **E0 prefix** of the scancode set 1 is translated by the portable table
  `kernel/input_set1.h` instead of being dropped: the arrows, Home, End,
  PgUp, PgDn, Insert and Delete are keys of the machine (69-78 of
  `enum k_input_key`, appended and never moved), the right Ctrl is a modifier,
  and the Enter and the slash of the keypad still type for the shell.

`make x86-vga-smoke` types two lines into the editor with `sendkey`, saves,
**reboots and reads the file back with `cat`**, and lists, moves and deletes in
the manager: **129 checks**, up from 88.

## The floating point of a task

Since `execve` of a program is asynchronous, two programs with a `double` can
compute at once. The switch of a task saves and loads the XMM and x87 state
now: 512 bytes of `struct port_task`, aligned by hand because `k_port_alloc`
gives 8 and `fxsave` wants 16, `fxsave64`/`fxrstor64` in `commit_switch()` -
the one place every switch of the machine passes. A `fork` copies the live
state into the child, an `execve` starts the new image from a clean template
(`FCW 037f`, `MXCSR 1f80`), and the trampoline of an application processor
turns `CR4.OSFXSR`/`OSXMMEXCPT` and `CR0.NE` on - without them SSE in ring 3 of
the second core was `#UD` and `fxsave` itself would fault.

`/bin/fpu2` is the test that failed before the fix: two children compute in
`xmm0`/`xmm1` while the parent computes, and a third dirties x87 and the
control words before an `execve`. Before: `child 3 0.142838110462` and
`child 7 0.000000000000`. After: the exact numbers and `FPU: state 037f 1f80`.

## The disk

The driver of the ATA port was a loop of 256 `inw`/`outw` per sector with a
hand-made byte swap, and the filesystem above it asked for a kilobyte at a
time. Both changed:

* `rep insl`/`rep outsl` (32-bit PIO: two words of the data register in one bus
  cycle) replace the loop of 16-bit port reads. The byte swap was never needed:
  the port is little-endian, the memory is little-endian, and a word of the
  drive is a word of the buffer. The 16-bit `rep insw` stays for IDENTIFY.
  A sector went from 15 to 9 microseconds;
* a **cache of sectors** under the filesystems (`components/kernel/src/blockdev.c`,
  16 sectors, memory taken from `kmm` lazily), read-through with a **read-ahead
  of 8**, and write-back on the device that asks for it. The metadata of a
  volume is rewritten on every block it allocates, and the cache turns those
  writes into one command of 16 sectors.

| Volume, file of 64 MiB | 1.4.0 | 1.5.0 |
|---|---|---|
| ext2, write | 1.34 MiB/s | **8.40 MiB/s** |
| ext2, read | 4.70 MiB/s | **13.80 MiB/s** |
| MicroFS, write | 1.35 MiB/s | **2.01 MiB/s** |
| MicroFS, read | 13.06 MiB/s | **17.31 MiB/s** |

`make x86-volume-smoke` (19 checks, two volumes of 5 GiB) went from 206 to
**85 seconds** and its oracle, `e2fsck -fn` of the host, is clean after a
session that never unmounted. A read-ahead of the whole cache was measured and
is worse (2 MiB/s of reading): it evicts the metadata.

What is left is the bus-master DMA - the port has no contract of a physical
address to hand the controller - and the allocator of MicroFS, which scans its
bitmap from the start for every block (about two thirds of the time of a write
on a volume of 5 GiB). Both are in `docs/debts.md`, and DMA is the first task of
1.6.

## What is not in 1.5

* the network server (an HTTP server, MQTT, an update from the network) and
  `mmap`: 1.6;
* accounts, `login` and `su`, a group in a filesystem record, the setuid bit of
  an image: the second half of the users;
* a mouse (the AUX channel of the 8042 and IRQ 12), a compositor and a window
  server: the devices of 2.0;
* the journal of ext2 and of MicroFS, a symbolic link, a name of a listing
  longer than 32 bytes;
* `O_EXCL` in the header of the library (the flag is in the ABI and in the
  kernel), `poll` (the next free number is 43);
* the lazy path of the FPU (`CR0.TS` and `#NM`) and `XSAVE`/AVX;
* the I/O APIC, the wake IPI of the scheduler and a guard page: the K8 table of
  `docs/debts.md`.
