# Micro OS 1.3

The release of the **many cores** and of the programs that needed them. The
x86-64 machine starts both of its processors, a task runs on either, and the
shell of a machine with two cores is the same shell; SQLite, zstd, `file` and
`awk` arrive as commands of `/bin`; the volume stops being bounded by the
memory of the kernel; ext2 and FAT32 can be written, and the installer knows
the license of what it installs.

The board stays the **compatibility port** of ADR 0018: it builds, passes its
device test and receives the cheap changes (16 descriptors, the ABI of
`ftruncate`, the FAT32 writer). Many cores are x86-64 and the host substrate.

## Many cores

`KERNEL_SMP` is off by default and on in the build the port tests. With it on,
the firmware describes two processors and the boot line says what is used:

```text
x86-64: 2 cpu(s) reported by the firmware, 2 in use (apic 0xfee00000)
cpu: 2 core(s) of the machine are used by the kernel
```

The work is the second phase of ADR 0012:

* **the big kernel lock**: `k_port_enter_critical()` is one atomic lock with
  the depth of the nesting, taken by the outermost entry on a core and left by
  it; the heap has its own, and the order is BKL then heap;
* **per-core state**: a GDT, a TSS with its IST stacks and the RSP0 of the
  ring, the `current` task, the pending-free list, the thread pointer, and the
  identity of a core read as one word of the GS record;
* **the AP**: a trampoline linked at `0x7000` and copied before the kernel
  runs, INIT-SIPI-SIPI through the ICR, and an entry in C that takes its own
  record and joins the scheduler;
* **the local APIC**: mapped once in the top 2 GiB and enabled on every core,
  a timer calibrated against the TSC (a 10 ms window gives the frequency), the
  IPI vector, and the count of the IPIs received per core;
* **the fixes the second core found**: `CR0.WP` was set by the boot core
  alone, so a write of the kernel through a read-only page did not fault on
  the AP and the copy-on-write of a program answered differently in alternate
  cycles; the console was atomic against the interrupts of one core and not
  against another; the heap lock did not turn the interrupts off and a tick
  could switch to a task that allocates; the children of a killed parent held
  their slots for ever; a task that finished printed after its state was
  published.

`make x86-smp-smoke` drives the shell and two workers on the two cores (34
checks), and `make soak-smp` is the 600-second session with the ticks of both
cores sampled every cycle: 223 cycles, 8 354 commands, no error, the heap and
the volume flat. The I/O APIC is still a stub - the 8259 delivers the timer
and the keyboard to the core of the boot - and that is the K8 table of
`docs/debts.md`.

`file` knows the format of this machine (`MOX1`) and looks inside a gzip and a
zstd frame; `zstd` reads a frame of the host and the host reads the frame of
the machine; `sqlite3` keeps its database on a MicroFS volume through a VFS of
the port; `awk` runs the POSIX scripts of `/etc`. The library of a program is
one implementation per name for all of them: the POSIX families of sbase, the
formatter with a point of Lua, the streams of 1.2.1.

## The contract of a program

* a task has **16 descriptors** (four in 1.2), which is what a database, a
  pipe of three stages and a program with a file of its own ask for;
* **`ftruncate` (35)** cuts a file in place: MFS and ext2 implement it, FAT32
  answers `ENOTSUP`, and `/bin/truncate` is the command that shows it;
* an image of a program may be 8 MiB, and a package of the installer may be
  the same 8 MiB (`PKG_PACKAGE_MAX` is the ceiling of the image).

## The volume, and the filesystems

MicroFS kept the map of its free blocks in a fixed array of two blocks, which
is 16 MiB of volume and no more. The map is now allocated for the volume the
mount finds: 2 KiB for the 16 MiB image of x86-64, 615 KiB for 5 GiB, and a
volume past 64 GiB is refused with a sentence instead of a guess. The write
path stopped counting the chain on every call as well, so a file is written in
time proportional to its blocks.

**ext2 is written** (ADR 0029): the bitmaps of the groups, the inodes, the
directories (a directory grows through an indirect block), create, write,
truncate, unlink, `mkdir`, `chmod`, `chown`, the times, and a `ext2_check()`
of the machine. The proof is not our own test: `mke2fs` on the host, mount,
write, unmount, `e2fsck -fn` and `debugfs` - and the other direction, a file
of 400 KiB with a double indirect block written by `debugfs`, read, cut and
deleted by the machine, `e2fsck` clean.

**FAT32 is complete**: FSInfo, the two copies of the table, long names,
`unlink`, `rename` over an existing file, `mkdir`, `rmdir`, the dates; the
same oracle - `fsck.fat`, `mdir`, `mcopy` - in both directions.

## The installer and the license

ADR 0025 is the boundary, and it is now a rule of the code: an entry of the
index carries `license`, `source` and `source_sha256`, and a package whose
license is `GPL-`, `LGPL-`, `AGPL-` or `CDDL-` without a source and the digest
of its archive is refused (`PKG_ELICENSE`). `pkg info` prints the license and
the source of a package of a repository and of an installed one. The first
copyleft package is the work of 1.4: `docs/porting-gpl.md` is its recipe.

## What is not in 1.3

* a root that is not MicroFS: ADR 0027 decides it, the mount that reads the
  type of the partition and the two images of the release are 1.4;
* the five copyleft programs, and the SDK that builds them: 1.4;
* the interface: ADR 0028 decides the two devices (`/dev/fb0`,
  `/dev/input0`) and the skeleton is 1.4;
* the I/O APIC, the wake IPI of the scheduler, the FPU state of a task and a
  guard page under the stacks: the K8 table of `docs/debts.md`;
* the journal of SQLite on a file: a task has 16 descriptors now, the port
  still asks for `MEMORY`.
