# Micro OS 1.8

> The release of the machine of a person with a laptop: the door of USB, the
> disk of a laptop under the rule that a medium this system did not format is
> read-only, a program of somebody else built with no patch, and a volume whose
> grain is its own.

1.7 made a laptop boot: a firmware of its own, an image of a CD and of a stick,
a desktop and a palette. What it did not make was a laptop that **works**: the
keyboard of the machine was the PS/2 of a firmware that a modern one no longer
shares, the disk of the machine was not seen at all, and a program of the
outside world had to be rewritten before it could run. 1.8 is that half.

## The door of USB (ADR 0040)

A machine of these years has no PS/2 port the firmware will share and no COM
port. What it has is xHCI, and 1.8 speaks it: the controller is found by its
class on the PCI bus, reset, and programmed with a command ring, an event ring
with its ERST, the slots and the device contexts, the ports with their speed
and their reset - all of it by the physical addresses of the contract of
ADR 0032.

Three classes on top of it:

* **a keyboard** of the HID boot protocol: eight-byte reports that the portable
  kernel decodes (`kernel/input_hid.c`), with the same events and the same
  characters as the PS/2 one, so a machine with both reads them as one
  keyboard and `/dev/input0` does not change;
* **a stick** of mass storage (BOT, class 08/06/0x50): `INQUIRY`, `READ
  CAPACITY(10)`, `READ(10)`, `WRITE(10)` and a disk of the block layer that can
  be the root of a machine;
* **a wire** of CDC-ECM: a dongle or a telephone as the network of a laptop
  without one.

`make usb-smoke` runs a machine with `i8042=off` - no PS/2 device exists, so a
key can only arrive through the controller - and `make usb-stick-smoke
USB_STICK=/dev/sdc` attaches the stick of the owner read-only and proves by the
digest of its head and the counter of the sectors the kernel gave it that not a
byte of it was written.

## The disk of a person, and the rule before the code (ADR 0041)

1.6 could read a volume of the module the boot carried, of virtio, of the IDE
channel of 1984. A laptop of these years has none of them: it has a SATA
controller of a chipset or a disk of NVMe. Both are in 1.8 (ADR 0042), and both
came **after** the rule that made them safe:

* a medium this system did not format is **read-only**, whatever it carries;
* a volume is writable only when its superblock carries our mark **and**
  `/etc/volumes` names it;
* a write below the first sector of a partition is refused;
* a foreign superblock (ext2, ext4, btrfs, NTFS, XFS, FAT32) is refused at the
  mount, with the name of the system that made it;
* the first boot with a controller of a disk says what it found and writes
  nothing.

The gates hash the whole image before and after the boot: the volume of this
system is refused a write and the hash does not move, and the volume of btrfs
stops the boot with the hash of the image unchanged. The disk of the owner, a
btrfs of a terabyte, is under that rule and no lane of 1.8 writes to it.

## The library of a program nobody rewrote

`popen`, `pclose` and `system` are a pipe, a fork, a dup2 and the execve of the
first word of a line. On them, `make ports-smoke` builds **thirteen utilities
of sbase with not one line changed** and runs them on the image of the release:

    true false echo basename dirname pwd printenv sleep cksum tee cmp whoami
    sha256sum

The proof is not a word: `tests/ports/upstream.txt` holds the digest of every
vendored file and the gate recalculates it before the machine starts, so a byte
of a local edit fails the gate without a boot. `docs/ports-table.md` counts, per
program, the lines of its own source, the lines of a port layer this system
adds and the lines of the upstream source that had to be edited: 577 / 0 / 0
for the thirteen.

## The grain of a volume (ADR 0043)

The sector of a disk is a property of the disk; the grain of a volume is a
property of the volume. `struct k_blockdev_view` is a device whose sector is the
one of the superblock over a device whose sector is smaller - arithmetic over
one call, no copy and no allocation - and ext2 opens one when its superblock
says a block of 4 KiB on a medium of 512-byte sectors. `e2fsck -fn` and
`debugfs` are clean on every path of a write at that grain, and a volume of
5 GiB is written and read at both.

The lane also found a defect of the read ahead of 1.6: the wait of the stream
was armed **inside** the call that read, so a write of 16 MiB at 4 KiB read
36 MiB of the disk before it wrote a byte. The same write now reads 2.5 MiB and
runs at 184 MiB/s instead of 129.

## The defects the release found

* the read ahead above - 36 MiB of reading for 16 MiB of writing, since 1.6;
* the descriptors 1 and 2 were "the console" only while nothing had opened
  them, so the first `pipe()` or `open()` of a program took them and `tee`
  wrote its answer into its own file (since 1.5);
* `telnet` asked for the password on the console of the machine instead of the
  socket of the session, which the cleanroom of 1.7 wrote down;
* a case of the process tests waited three seconds for a program it had started
  as a task among the others, and answered a failure of the property on a busy
  host instead of a slow machine;
* `make test-tsan` did not compile (a variable of a case under the same
  condition as its use) and `test-minimal` was red - two gates that nobody ran,
  which are the two that a release of 1.8 now runs on every push.

## What is not in 1.8

* the **write path of the two controllers of M2** (`WRITE`, `Flush`, `mount -w`)
  if the lane of K14 lands after this release: until it does, a disk of a
  laptop is read-only by the rule and by the absence of the code;
* **hubs of the USB** (class 9), RNDIS, a hot-plug after the boot and the
  interrupt of the xHCI controller while the event ring is polled;
* a **terminal** with `termios`, the second half of the desktop (M5);
* the **setuid bit of `MOX1`** and a group record (M6), which are format
  decisions;
* **Ethernet** on a laptop (M7) and Wi-Fi (`docs/plan-wifi.md`);
* the **ARM port** (`docs/plan-arm64.md`, no date).
